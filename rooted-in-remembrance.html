What the site photos changed

Three things in your photos overrule the usual approach to a memorial planting, and all three point the same direction: go smaller, go slower, and put the tree somewhere it can actually live.

Revised after site review

These are raised planters, not beds

Every orange mark sits on top of a masonry wall — the entry planter by the steps, the wedge above the walk, the strip on the wall cap. Soil volume is capped, the sides shed heat, and roots have nowhere to run.

Confirm depth before specifying anything woody

They're small

Scaled against the handrails and wall courses, the three zones come to roughly 22, 30 and 30 sq ft. That's a specimen and an underplanting in each — not a garden bed.

Field-measure to confirm

The native tree can't go in them

A dogwood or redbud needs real root volume and will strangle in a planter box. The native memorial tree belongs in the ground-level bed just beyond the wall; the planter carries the specimen maple at eye level.

04

Three planting schemes

Each pairs a weeping Japanese maple, sized to survive a planter box, with a tree native to the Virginia coastal plain planted in the ground bed beyond the wall. The entry gets direct sun until roughly midday and building shade after — which is precisely why a laceleaf maple works here at all. Afternoon shade is the reason its leaves won't scorch in a zone 7b August.

05

What maturity actually costs you

Specimens at full size against the entry canopy, all at one scale. The common failure is planting for the ribbon-cutting photo and discovering in year twelve that the canopy is in the glazing and the maple has been sheared flat to keep the handrail clear.

  • Measure the soil, not the opening

    A planter that reads generous on top may be 18 in. deep over slab. Confirm depth and whether it drains before a single woody plant is specified — everything else here depends on that number.

  • Irrigation isn't optional

    Raised masonry dries from all sides. Drip on a timer, or accept that the specimen dies in its second August. Budget it as part of the installation, not as maintenance.

  • Let it spill, on purpose

    A mature laceleaf is wider than it is tall. In a planter that's a feature, not a fault — site it off-center so the cascade falls over the stone face rather than into the handrail.

  • Keep the native tree out of the box

    Dogwood, serviceberry and fringetree all need real root volume. Ground bed beyond the wall, minimum 8 ft off the glazing so mature limbs never touch it.

  • Plan for the deer

    Pressure is high here. Ferns, sedge, hellebore, amsonia and inkberry are reliably passed over. Anything at planter height is at browsing height — the raised bed helps, but won't protect a young maple.

  • Protect the sight lines

    The entry planter sits at the top of the steps. Nothing that grows into the handrail approach or blocks the view down the stair — this is a walked threshold before it's a garden.

06

Naming the event

It has to carry gravity without tipping into sentiment, survive being said out loud every year, and work equally well on a bronze plaque and an internal calendar invite.

Rooted in Remembrance

An annual planting in honor of the patients who guide our work

The Living Tribute

Warmer and more ceremonial. Best if the program leans toward family attendance and spoken remembrance.

Where Roots Remember

The most lyrical option. Strong on signage, slightly soft for a recurring internal calendar hold.

Three Springs

Names the three-year arc directly, which fits this site exactly. It also has a built-in ending — which may or may not be what you want.

07

Getting it in the ground

Planting windows

  • Oct–NovBest window in 7b–8a. Roots establish all winter with no heat stress. Plant here if the event date is flexible.
  • Mar–early AprSecond-best, and the only window that puts dogwood, serviceberry and fringetree in bloom on the day itself.
  • Jun–AugHigh risk in a raised planter. If the month is fixed, use container stock, pre-soak at the nursery for three weeks, and run drip through October.

Before the shovels

  • −6 wkProbe the planters for actual soil depth and drainage, and get landlord or facilities sign-off. This is the gate — everything downstream assumes it.
  • −5 wkVirginia 811 locate for the ground-level tree pit. Soil test the ground bed; the planters get fresh specified mix regardless.
  • −4 wkTag the actual maple at the nursery. Never accept substitution on the specimen — the whole planting reads off that one plant.
  • −1 wkContractor pre-excavates and backfills. The ceremonial planting should be a real act, not an hour of labor in front of guests.

Day of

  • SetupPlanter pre-mulched, specimen staged beside an open pit, soil in labeled vessels for guests to place.
  • ProgramFifteen minutes. Welcome, reading, the planting itself, then quiet time. Resist the urge to add speakers.
  • MarkerBronze plate set into the stone cap, year and dedication line only. Each year adds a plate in its own zone.
  • AftercareAssign a named owner for two years of watering. This is where memorial plantings die — not at installation.
Compliance — worth settling early Keep the garden and all signage free of product or brand marks so the installation stays clearly non-promotional. Patient names or images need written consent on file, and honoring named individuals is worth routing past legal and medical review before plaque copy is set. If patients or families attend, brief staff that the day involves no product discussion, and have photography releases ready at check-in.
