01

The order of ceremony

Six movements over the lunch hour. Select one to see its direction, script or reading. The plaque is staked last, after the garden exists and after it has been read to and played to — which is also what sets up the silence.

02

Making the planting work

Twenty minutes, sixty untrained people, and a bed that has to look deliberate afterwards. This is the segment that fails if nobody plans it.

The day before

Crew preps, nobody digs

Strip spent summer material, top up with fresh planter mix, water thoroughly, let it settle overnight. Wet soil on the morning of is heavy and cold on the hands.

  • Fresh mix to within 2 in. of the cap
  • Slow-release feed worked in
  • Water the night before, not the morning of
Ninety minutes before

Set every pot in its final spot

The highest-leverage item on this list. Crew places all 290 pots, still potted, at 6–7 in. centres in the exact layout. Guests never make a spacing decision, so the bed reads as designed instead of as a crowd.

  • 6–7 in. centres, staggered rows
  • Colour drifts pre-sorted by the crew
  • Marshals keep spares out of sight
Kit

Enough tools, not too many

One trowel between two people keeps it social and stops the beds getting crowded. Gloves offered, never required — a lot of people will want to feel the soil.

  • 30 hand trowels, 60 pairs of gloves
  • 6 watering cans, filled and staged
  • Hand-wash station and towels at the exit
Access

The raised planters are a gift

All three zones sit at roughly waist height, so a wheelchair user or anyone who can't kneel participates on the same terms as everyone else. Worth saying out loud in the instructions.

  • Clear 4 ft approach at the entry planter
  • Lowest-reach run reserved for anyone seated
  • Two folding stools available, unremarked
Flow

Split across three zones

Sixty people at one 22 sq ft planter is a scrum. Marshals walk thirds of the group to the wedge and the wall strip as the music starts, so all three plant at once.

  • Zone 1 entry planter — 20 people, 80 plants
  • Zone 2 the wedge — 20 people, 105 plants
  • Zone 3 wall strip — 20 people, 105 plants
Noon heat

The hour works against the plants

Midday sun on warm September soil is the worst window for pansies. It's fixable, but it has to be planned rather than hoped.

  • Water beds hard the night before and an hour ahead
  • Keep staged flats in full shade until the last minute
  • Water in immediately, then again at end of day
  • Water station for guests, not just for the garden

The plant order

Two colours, not a mix. A restrained white-and-violet planting reads as deliberate; a rainbow flat reads as a garden centre — and on a memorial planting that difference is the whole thing. Order the two cultivars separately so the crew can drift them rather than mixing on site.

Spacing

6–7 inches on centre

Closer than a nursery will tell you. Pansies are annuals here — they never need room to grow into, and a tight planting looks finished on the day rather than in six weeks.

Timing

Three weeks early, and workable

Ideal is mid-October with soil near 60°F; September 22 will be closer to 70°F. Use heat-tolerant series only, skip high-nitrogen feed at planting, and expect a light shear in November to fix stretch.

Aftercare

Deadhead in February

One pass pulling spent flowers in late February roughly doubles the March display. Assign it to a named person now — this is the step that always gets dropped.

05

Open items

  • Reading names aloud is a legal question, not a creative one

    Your deck has a question mark on it, and rightly. Posthumous use of ambassador names — spoken and cast in bronze — needs written consent from families or estates plus legal and medical review. Start now; six months is not excessive. Verify every spelling against a family-confirmed source.

  • Decide the plaque growth model

    Fixed dedication header with annual name plates added each year, or a single plaque recast annually. Plates are cheaper, scale indefinitely, and make each year's addition its own small ceremony.

  • A stake won't hold in a raised planter

    Those beds are 18–24 in. over structure. A staked plaque in shallow mix leans within a season. Pre-install a hidden anchor sleeve at the front edge so the ceremonial staking goes in clean and stays plumb.

  • Deer will eat these

    Pansies are among the most heavily browsed bedding plants there is, and pressure on this site is high. Budget repellent from the morning after planting, re-applied every three to four weeks and after rain. Don't spray during the ceremony.

  • Gratitude or remembrance

    The deck names the event Rooted in Gratitude, which is warmer than a memorial frame. If the ambassadors being named have died, the welcome and closing copy need to hold both — thanks for what they gave, and the fact that they're gone. Worth a deliberate decision rather than drifting between the two.

  • Keep it non-promotional

    No product or brand marks on signage or in the garden. If families attend, brief staff that the day involves no product discussion, and have photography releases at check-in.
